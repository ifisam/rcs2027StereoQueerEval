The course

Research Case Studies is a compulsory 15 LP project module of the M.Sc. Natural Language Processing, recommended for the 3rd semester. There are no prerequisites. This year the whole group works on one real research problem, a SemEval shared task, from the first baseline to a peer-reviewed system paper.

  • Supervision: Iftakharul Islam (co-supervisor)
  • Group: 8 to 9 students, organised in small teams
  • Goal: official runs in the January 2027 evaluation window and a submitted system description paper in February 2027
  • Registration deadline: 31 December 2026. Missing it results in 5.0, and there is no submission in the summer semester.

The shared task

StereoQueerEval is Task 8 of SemEval 2027, in the track Stereotypes, Framing, and Sentiment Analysis. Systems classify YouTube comments, using the video title and description as context.

Subtask A · binary Stereotype detection Does the comment contain a stereotype about LGBTQIA+ people?
Subtask B · multiclass Hate speech classification Explicit hate, implicit hate, or non-hateful and supportive content.
Subtask C · multilabel Target and identity For hateful comments only: the target type (individual or group) and the LGBTQIA+ identities referenced.

Languages: English, Italian and Dutch, plus Persian as a low-resource language that appears only in the test data.

Organisers: Alessandra Teresa Cignarella, Marzieh Abdolmaleki and Els Lefever (Ghent University), Greta Damo (Université Côte d'Azur), Marta Marchiori Manerba (University of Turin) and Debora Nozza (Bocconi University). Full description: task page. The data contains offensive language.

How to participate

We take part in the shared task as one group. The class registers and submits as a single team, so not everyone needs to register or submit individually; the steps below are done once for the group.

  1. Sign the Data Usage Agreement. Within 24 hours you are added to the StereoQueerEval Google Group and receive the password for the data archive.
  2. Download the data and annotation guidelines from the StereoQueerEval GitHub repository.
  3. Build and evaluate your system on the training data, and report progress every week.
  4. Submit your predictions on Codabench during the evaluation period (the competition link will be announced by the organisers).
  5. Write the system description paper: your approach, the algorithms, models and resources used, your experiments, and an analysis of the results.

Details: how to participate. Updates from the organisers arrive through the Google Group.

ECTS and workload

In the German system, one ECTS credit (Leistungspunkt, LP) stands for 25 to 30 hours of total student work. A full-time semester is 30 LP. Workload includes meetings, reading, implementation, experiments, writing and exam preparation, not only contact hours.

Weekly progress meetings

The whole group meets with the supervisor once a week. Each team shows what it did since the last meeting, what it found, and what comes next. Meeting times will be announced.

Research Case Studies is a project seminar, so attendance is compulsory. Regular participation is still certified if you miss up to three sessions, and no more than six teaching hours, in the semester (APO § 5 (5)). If you cannot attend, tell the supervisor in advance.

Assessment: the portfolio

The module is assessed only by a portfolio examination (Portfolioprüfung).

  • Content: an introduction, a collection of documents covering the whole module period (code, experiment logs, progress reports, the paper), and a reflection.
  • Time: four weeks to select and compile the documents and write the introduction and reflection.
  • Declaration: a signed statement that you compiled the portfolio yourself and used no sources or aids other than those stated.
  • Submission: digital submission (for example as a presentation) is possible with the examiner's prior consent.
  • Registration: binding, in the campus management system - PORTA, by 31 December 2026.

Where points can be reduced

Under the examination regulations

  1. Plagiarism, undeclared AI-generated text or code, or any other deception: the assessment is graded 5.0 (APO § 18 (4)). A second proven case can lead to loss of enrolment.
  2. Not registering by 31 December 2026: graded 5.0, with no submission in the summer semester.
  3. Missing a graded deliverable without a valid reason: graded 5.0 (APO § 18 (1)).
  4. Missing more than three sessions or six hours of the project seminar: regular participation is not certified (APO § 5 (5)).
  5. Not reporting illness or another valid reason immediately and in writing, with a medical certificate for illness: the absence counts as unexcused (APO § 18 (2)).
  6. Submitting the portfolio after the deadline without an approved extension: treated as not submitted.

Examination rules at a glance

  • Grades: 1.0 to 4.0 is a pass and 5.0 is a fail. Module grades keep only the first decimal, without rounding (§ 16).
  • Repeats: a failed module exam can be repeated up to twice, within six semesters. Failing the last repeat means the Master's examination is definitively failed (§ 17).
  • Deregistration: possible until 24:00 the day before the examination, without giving reasons (§ 11 (6)).
  • Disruption: a student who disrupts an examination can be excluded, and the assessment counts as 5.0 (§ 18 (4)).
  • Review: within four weeks you can ask the Examination Committee to review a decision on deception or disruption (§ 18 (5)).
  • Accommodations: students with disabilities, chronic illness, children or care duties can apply to the chair of the Examination Committee (§ 3 (6)).
  • Inspection: apply in writing to the examiner within one year of the result (§ 22).

Binding text: General Examination Regulations for the Master's Degree Programmes at Trier University (12 November 2007, as amended 1 August 2025), and the subject regulations for the M.Sc. Natural Language Processing. If this page and the regulations differ, the regulations apply.
